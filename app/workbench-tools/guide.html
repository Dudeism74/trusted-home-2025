Mobile workbench tool fit guide

The 84 × 36-inch bench uses a lowered table-saw deck, a recessed miter station and a planer that feeds across the bench. Compare the measurements below with the tools you own or are considering before you cut their supports. The linked models are examples. Fit them to your bench with the worksheet on sheet 2.

Sheets 2–5 and 11

Lay out the frame

Tape the 84 × 36-inch footprint onto the floor. Record each machine’s mounting pattern and working height. Mark the miter swing, the table saw’s two positions and the planer’s feed path on cardboard. A long straight board makes it easy to see where stock would meet the next station.

When the base is dry-fitted, compare its two corner-to-corner diagonals. Use the actual caster plates to mark their bolt holes, checking for clearance from posts, edges, washers and the wheel swivel.

Sheets 2, 5 and 7

Set the table-saw deck

Measure from the saw’s feet to its table. Subtract that height, the carrier thickness and any leveling shims from the 42-inch working height. The resulting dimension is the top of the lowered deck. Allow room around the mounting holes for washers, pins and hold-downs.

The example leaf projects 24 inches. With the tool unplugged, mock up its travel and the width of your workpiece. Check the fence extension, guard and miter-bar paths in both positions. At the outer position, plan where the outfeed stand will support a long board.

Sheets 2 and 6

Size the miter pocket

The drawn pocket is 25 inches wide and 22 inches deep, open to the front. Trace the saw’s full sweep with its hose attached and check clearance behind the fence. Verify that the mounting bolts land on supported shelf material and that you can reach the nuts.

Measure from the saw’s feet to the surface that supports the board. With a 3½-inch stock-bed height and zero shims, the shelf top would sit 38½ inches above the floor. Use a long straightedge across both wings and the saw bed before securing it.

Sheets 2, 5 and 8

Check the planer opening

The example provides 22½ inches between post faces. WEN lists the example machine’s overall dimensions as 22 × 23½ × 18¼ inches. Its 12½-inch cutting capacity describes the board width. Measure its housing in the intended orientation, including the crank and hose, before you commit to the post spacing.

Allow room for the head through its full travel, access to the cutter cover and a path for the board through both long faces. If a tool needs more space, revise the neighboring bays on the editable layout before cutting the frame.

Sheets 8 and 10

Match the dust fittings

Measure the tool port and both mating hose cuffs. The example DEWALT vacuum comes with a nominal 1⅞-inch hose, while the Oneida separator uses 2½-inch fittings. Check which ends fit inside or over one another before choosing an adapter. The separator also needs a compatible five-gallon pail.

In the center bay, leave clearance to remove the lid and filter, and enough hose slack for the saw to move onto the shelf. Consult each tool’s manual for the required extraction capacity. The planer may call for a larger collector.

Sheets 10 and 11

Fit the removable router box

Choose the insert plate and confirm compatibility with the router base you intend to mount. Use the plate maker’s template and fasteners. Size the box around the motor, handles, adjustment access and cord. If the top shares the bench’s working height, its outside height is 42 inches minus the actual saw-deck height.

Mock up the box beside the table saw with the fence rails extended. Check the saw’s lateral travel and leave room to remove the router box when the two stations interfere.
